# Predictable Revenue: Lead Generation & Qualification Framework
Based on the principles from Aaron Ross and Marylou Tyler's book *Predictable Revenue*
,
building a scalable sales pipeline requires distinguishing between how contacts are acquired
and how they are qualified.
## Part 1: The Three Avenues of Acquisition
A healthy business diversifies its acquisition strategy across three distinct channels:
### 1. Seeds (Word of Mouth & Organic)
Seeds are highly qualified opportunities that grow organically through customer success, strong
relationships, and network effects.
* **The Approach:** Delivering exceptional results so clients refer others, or building deep trust
within a community.
* **Example:** A logistics client in Colombia sees massive efficiency gains from a reinforcement
learning integration and recommends the platform to another founder in their network.
* **Trade-offs:** These boast the highest conversion rates and fastest closing times, but they
are slow to cultivate and difficult to scale predictably.
### 2. Nets (Inbound Marketing)
Nets involve casting a wide net to capture a large volume of potential interest (one-to-many
marketing).
* **The Approach:** Content marketing, SEO, webinars, and digital advertising.
* **Example:** Publishing a Spanish-language whitepaper on *"Cómo optimizar procesos en
PYMES con Inteligencia Artificial"* and capturing the contact information of business owners
who download it.
* **Trade-offs:** Highly scalable and excellent for filling the top of the funnel quickly, but these
contacts typically have lower conversion rates and require extensive nurturing.
### 3. Spears (Outbound Prospecting)
Spears represent targeted, one-to-one outbound sales efforts where you identify and hunt
specific accounts.
* **The Approach:** Dedicated Sales Development Representatives (SDRs) conducting
researched, personalized outreach to specific decision-makers.
* **Example:** Building a list of COOs at 50 specific mid-sized manufacturing companies in
Spain, and contacting them directly with a tailored pitch regarding supply chain bottlenecks.
* **Trade-offs:** This provides highly predictable revenue and precise control over target clients,
but it demands significant effort, resilience, and dedicated personnel.

## Part 2: Redefining the "Lead"
A core tenet of *Predictable Revenue* is that a raw list of names or email addresses is **not** a
list of leads. Treating them as such wastes the time of expensive closers (Account Executives).
Contacts must be filtered through a strict qualification process:
### 1. Suspects (or Inquiries)
* **Definition:** Raw data with little to no demonstrated intent.
* **Context:** An email captured from a "Net" strategy or a target on a "Spear" list. At this stage,
it is merely contact information.
### 2. Prospects
* **Definition:** A suspect that has been actively researched and matches the Ideal Customer
Profile (ICP).
* **Context:** You know they fit the target demographic on paper, but their current needs,
budget, or timeline remain unknown.
### 3. Qualified Leads (Sales Qualified Leads / SQLs)
* **Definition:** The only contact that deserves to be called a true "Lead.
"
* **Context:** A prospect graduates to a Qualified Lead only after an SDR has held a
conversation, confirmed a tangible business need (e.g., a specific bottleneck the reinforcement
learning solution can solve), and secured an agreement for a next step, such as a product
demo.
### The Handoff
The separation of roles is crucial. Prospectors (SDRs) handle Suspects and Prospects, filtering
out the noise. They only hand off **Qualified Leads** to the Closers (Account Executives). This
specialization ensures that Account Executives spend 100% of their time selling to companies
that actually have the budget and need to deploy the solution.
